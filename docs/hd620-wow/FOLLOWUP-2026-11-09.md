# Evening return comparisons — 2026-11-09

Follow-up to [the profiling report](PROFILING.md). **Stable 40 FPS has not been achieved.** These are experiments on a Kaby Lake HD620 laptop, not general recommendations for other Intel generations.

The game remains experimental: the narrowly scoped compatibility override and fail-closed bound-resource guard stay enabled. No unsupported shader read was implemented, hidden, or skipped. No game shader replacement, dispatch suppression, math relaxation, or barrier removal was used. The immutable baseline release is unchanged.

## Ordinary DXVK 3.1 → 2.7 → 3.1

Same guarded candidates, Proton family, 1280×720 XWayland output, 853×480 internal resolution, AA off, bilinear upscale, 40 FPS cap and vsync off. The character is stationary at the same inn/bridge-facing location. Each process had a fresh private Mesa cache and warm-up before logging. GPU tracing, native compiler dumps, forced SIMD and MangoHud were disabled for these measurements.

| Candidate | Engine rows | Elapsed seconds | Mean FPS | Mean frame ms | Mean GPU render ms |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3.1 A | 103 | 103.764 | 20.720 | 48.257 | 46.400 |
| 2.7 B | 137 | 139.349 | **22.387** | **44.679** | **42.689** |
| 3.1 return | 115 | 116.187 | 20.637 | 48.461 | 46.802 |

All rows report GPU-bound. Relative to the arithmetic mean of the two 3.1 run means, 2.7 is approximately **8.3% faster** in this comparison. This supports keeping the already installed 2.7 candidate. These are approximately one-second engine aggregates, **not per-frame samples, 1% lows, or evidence of a stable FPS floor**.

Limitations: the online scene is not a deterministic replay; other players vary. Firefox was visible idle behind the game in the first A, but minimized in B and the return. The return reduces that particular concern but does not remove every background/thermal/scene confound. Do not claim a precisely isolated universal 8.3% improvement.

## Opt-in Mesa scheduler experiment: rejected

The [source patch, reproduction notes and aggregate evidence](mesa-scheduler/README.md) are included as a negative-result research record. The default-off flag evaluates existing dependency-preserving pre-register-allocation schedules; it does not change shader math, barriers, capabilities or SIMD policy. Actual hot-kernel widths were checked rather than assumed.

Same patched Mesa 26.2.6, flag OFF → ON → OFF: **44.978 → 45.183 → 45.228 ms** across 415 / 418 / 421 complete intrusive-trace frames, with zero complete-frame pairing errors. One hot kernel demonstrably changed top-down → latency-sensitive → top-down; all three retained their SIMD widths, instruction/send counts, GRFs and zero spills/fills. **No observed performance gain; rejected, default OFF.** The small differences do not establish statistical significance.

A separate original integer workload passed 18 exact native GPU readbacks across OFF/ON/OFF. This is limited integer coverage, not floating-point, typed-image, private-game-shader or comprehensive compiler validation. The driver experiment was isolated from system packages and was not selected for the ordinary launcher.

No GPU tracing, compiler dump or forced SIMD was enabled. This view differs from the earlier DXVK/Mesa comparisons: **do not compare its absolute FPS against the earlier approximately 22 FPS result**.

Only `intel_pstate/no_turbo` changed, **0 → 1 → 0**. The GPU's 300–1050 MHz limits and 1050 MHz boost, `max_perf_pct=100`, `balance_performance` EPP and AC power stayed unchanged. CPU scaling maximum changed as expected from 3500 to 2700 and back to 3500 MHz. There was a 30-second settling period before B and before the return. ## GPU clock controls: no exposed overclocking headroom

A subsequent **read-only** inventory on this laptop reported both `Max non-overclocked (RP0) frequency: 1050MHz` and `Max overclocked frequency: 1050MHz` in i915's frequency diagnostics. The configured maximum and boost were already 1050 MHz, the minimum 300 MHz, and the request 1050 MHz while instantaneous achieved readings were 950–1000 MHz. Up/down thresholds were 95/85%. The three preceding full captures likewise showed a constant 1050 MHz sysfs request.

The diagnostic's “overclocked” label denotes the driver's hardware/OC ceiling; it does **not** mean this laptop is overclocked. No higher ceiling is exposed here. The [corresponding i915 implementation in pinned Linux v6.18](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v6.18/drivers/gpu/drm/i915/gt/intel_rps.c#L2404-L2424) distinguishes this ceiling, the current request and achieved frequency. Its [minimum setter](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v6.18/drivers/gpu/drm/i915/gt/intel_rps.c#L2554-L2589) changes a soft floor and clamps the request; it does not override hardware power/thermal decisions. These source references explain the interface semantics, not a claim that v6.18 is the running kernel.

A higher floor might affect transient ramp-down, but with a sustained maximum request it cannot request more than 1050 MHz or force the achieved clock to that value. Raising the floor was therefore **not applied** as a cosmetic “overclock.” The existing 300/1050/1050 MHz minimum/maximum/boost and 95/85 thresholds remain unchanged. No voltage adjustment, register write, firmware modification or thermal-limit bypass was attempted. The request/actual gap alone does not establish its cause or prove throttling.
